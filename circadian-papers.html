Protein quality control helps set the pace of the fruit fly clock

Peer-reviewed · Geisseova et al., PNAS, Nov. 19, 2026

Geisseova and colleagues found that disrupting Caliban-dependent ribosome quality control delayed the buildup of the clock protein PERIOD. The flies also showed longer, weaker activity rhythms in constant darkness. The study connects the cell’s handling of stalled ribosomes to the timing of the circadian clock.

What chronic jet lag leaves behind in the brain

Peer-reviewed · Kubištová et al., Frontiers in Neuroscience, Nov. 19, 2026

After six months of weekly shifts in the light–dark cycle, rats spent a week back on a stable schedule. At that point, only 3 of 21 gene-by-region profiles that were rhythmic in controls still had a detectable daily rhythm. Hippocampal Per2 was the only one of the three that retained both its amplitude and peak timing.

Why it matters: A week on a stable schedule did not restore most of the measured rhythms outside the brain’s central clock.

Keep in mind: These were bulk tissue measurements in male rats. A weaker tissue rhythm could reflect weaker rhythms in individual cells, cells falling out of sync, or both.
